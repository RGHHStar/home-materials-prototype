# EverDwell Home Material Guide Prototype

This is a static website prototype that runs without installation.

## How to open it

Open `index.html` in Chrome, Edge, or Safari.

## Current features

- English by default, with a complete French option
- A 40-question household environmental impact assessment, shown one question per page
- Slider controls for frequency and degree questions, with option cards for categorical questions
- Assessment results with an overall level, category scores, and priority review areas
- An interactive home builder for insulation, exterior walls, windows, and roofing
- Existing-home material identification from names or descriptions
- Three suggested matches with an explicit uncertainty notice
- Details covering environment, health, insulation, durability, fire performance, cost, benefits, limitations, and safety
- Four sample materials: fibreglass, mineral wool, spray foam, and cellulose
- A local, credited reference photo for the home page and every sample material
- A three-level Sustainable Building Plan with 20 house systems and 166 curated options
- Unified detail pages with a short description, 0–100 sustainability ring, collapsible four-part rating, and indicative Canadian pricing
- Sustainability labels such as FSC-certified, reclaimed, recycled-content, bio-based, and low-VOC are treated as attributes instead of duplicate menu items
- Responsive desktop and mobile layouts

## Pricing basis

Prices are broad Canadian installed-cost estimates in CAD per square foot. Actual quotes vary by province, R-value, thickness, site access, and project size. They are not purchasing or contractor quotations.

Canadian pricing was spot-checked against current public market guides, including HomeStars siding ranges, Clean Energy Canada heat-pump research, Canadian residential solar installation guides, and Home Depot Canada retail listings. Broad category ranges are used because individual products and labour conditions vary substantially.

## Current scope

The Learn and Personal Action Plan modules are intentionally deferred to a later phase.
